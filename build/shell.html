Required columns: category, gender, base_salary, variable_pay, tenure_years, grade. Optional: fte (default 1.0), months_worked (default 12). Comma or semicolon separated.

Method : every quantity in the order it is computed

1. Normalisation

Part-time and partial-year employees are scaled to a full-year, full-time equivalent before any gap is measured, so that a 0.5 FTE employee is not mistaken for an underpaid one.

normalised_pay = pay / fte / (months_worked / 12) total_pay = base_salary + variable_pay (both normalised)

Cost is converted back into actual money at the end: a raise for a 0.5 FTE employee costs the company half.

2. Raw gap

Within each category, the mean and the median of total pay are taken for men and for women. A positive gap favours men; a negative gap favours women.

raw_gap_% = (mean_M − mean_F) / mean_M × 100 median gap computed the same way on medians

The gap on the variable component is computed and shown separately. The Directive requires it to be disclosed in its own right, and it exposes the common case where base salaries are level and the gap sits in bonuses.

3. Explained and unexplained

Within each category an ordinary least squares regression of total pay on grade and tenure, solving the coefficients directly from the normal equations without a library. The part of the gap predicted by differences in grade and tenure is the explained part; what remains is the unexplained residual. Only the residual is remediated.

total_pay ≈ intercept + coef_grade × grade + coef_tenure × tenure_years explained_% = gap predicted by the grade and tenure difference between men and women unexplained_% = raw_gap_% − explained_%

This is the only place in the tool where the word regression is used. Elsewhere the two quantities are named plainly: explained by grade and tenure and unexplained residual.

4. Recipients

One method only: employees of the underpaid gender whose pay is below the median of their own category. The increase is distributed in proportion to how far each of them falls short of that median.

shortfall_i = max(0, median_category − pay_i) share_i = shortfall_i / Σ shortfall

5. The two scenarios

Both are computed at once, on the same data. They differ only in where they stop.

  1. Minimum compliance: raise pay until the category's unexplained residual falls to the threshold. Categories already below the threshold are not touched at all.
  2. Full equalisation: raise pay until the residual reaches zero, in every category.

The gap between the two figures comes almost entirely from the categories that minimum compliance leaves alone, and there are usually more of those than expected.

Categories where the gap favours women are not adjusted in either scenario. Equalisation is upward only. They stay visible in the table and on the chart.

6. Employer contributions

Computed on the increase in base pay, not on the original salary, and split at the annual contribution ceiling.

contributions = rate_below × min(raise, max(0, ceiling − base_before)) + rate_above × max(0, base_before + raise − max(ceiling, base_before))

One formula covers a hard ceiling, a reduced rate above it, and a flat rate. Its visible consequence: in high-paid categories equalisation costs less than intuition suggests, because the part of the raise above the ceiling does not carry full contributions.

Only the first band of the Spanish solidarity contribution is modelled. The recipients of adjustments sit below the median of their category, so they are overwhelmingly below the ceiling or just above it; modelling all three bands would complicate the formula for an effect on a handful of employees.

7. Base pay or variable pay

The gap is measured on total pay and remediated through base salary.

The model applies the adjustment to base salary: this locks the correction in, whereas a one-off bonus is redistributed from scratch in the next cycle. A company is entitled to decide otherwise, closing the gap through the variable component or through a combination of the two. That decision changes both the cost (variable pay usually does not carry the full set of contributions) and how durable the result is. The model does not make that choice for the user.

The second-order effect is not modelled: a higher base salary raising the future bonus base, where the bonus is a percentage of salary.

8. Annual effect

With implementation in month m, the current year carries only the remaining months; the following year carries the full amount.

cost_current_year = full_amount × (13 − m) / 12 cost_next_year = full_amount

9. Totals

Euros in the current year, euros in the following year, and the uplift to the payroll as a percentage of total pay before adjustment.

payroll_uplift_% = total_cost / payroll_before × 100

10. Reliability flag

A category with fewer than 10 employees, or with fewer than 3 of either gender, is marked statistically unreliable. The figures are still shown, but they should not carry a decision on their own.

Not in scope
